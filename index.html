02 / Workflow

From radar observations to a land map

Multi-temporal radar observations can capture seasonal changes in vegetation and surface conditions, including under most cloud cover.

01

Build the time series

Sentinel-1 GRD C-band SAR observations are filtered by study area, date, acquisition mode, polarization and orbit settings. Early-, mid- and late-season composites represent the Kharif period.

02

Train the model

A Random Forest classifier uses radar-derived predictors and labelled reference polygons to distinguish agricultural from non-agricultural land.

03

Validate the result

Held-out reference polygons provide independent validation samples. Confusion-matrix metrics help assess overall and class-wise classification performance.

Input data

Data and features

  • Sentinel-1 GRD C-band SAR imagery
  • VV and VH backscatter polarizations
  • Early-, mid- and late-season composites
  • Reference polygons with class labels
  • Random Forest supervised classification

The current workflow summary reports 13 Sentinel-1 scenes: 4 early-season, 4 mid-season and 5 late-season scenes.

Remote sensing concept

Why use radar?

Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) measures microwave energy returned from the Earth's surface. VV and VH backscatter can respond to vegetation structure, surface moisture, roughness and field conditions.

Observations from multiple dates can reveal seasonal differences that may help separate broad land classes.

Radar signatures are influenced by multiple environmental factors and do not uniquely identify a crop species.

What do the results mean?

The overall accuracy is 83.06% for the independent validation sample. The agricultural class has higher user's accuracy, while its producer's accuracy is lower. Some reference agricultural samples were therefore missed by the model.

Additional metric

Cohen's kappa: 0.645

Kappa describes agreement beyond chance under its assumptions. Interpretation should consider reference quality, sample representativeness and spatial dependence.

04 / Interpretation

Limitations

  • Mixed pixels and fragmented agricultural fields.
  • Backscatter variations due to moisture, roughness and crop structure.
  • Potential errors in reference labels or polygon boundaries.
  • Spatial dependence between pixels from the same field.
  • Mapped-area estimates may be affected by classification errors.
Project notes

Frequently asked questions

Does the map identify individual crops?

No. The current model separates agricultural from non-agricultural land. Crop-specific mapping would require suitable crop labels, a multi-class model and independent validation.

Why use multiple dates?

Agricultural and surface conditions change through the growing season. Multi-date observations can capture differences that may not be visible in a single image.

Can mapped area be treated as ground truth?

No. Check the study boundary, pixel scale, masks, class values and classification errors before reporting area totals.

What should be checked before submission?

Verify the boundary, season, radar filters, class labels, training-validation split, accuracy results, area calculations and source citations.
